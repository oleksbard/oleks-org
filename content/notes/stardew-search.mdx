---

I play modded Stardew Valley. That means three wikis open in three tabs (the
official one, Stardew Valley Expanded, and Ridgeside Village), and a constant
low-grade panic about which one has the answer to "wait, who likes battery
packs again?"

[Stardew Search](/games/stardew) is my fix for that. One search box, all three
wikis at once, with a short AI summary up top.

The interesting bit isn't the parallel fan-out; that's just three fetches.
It's the query rewriting. A naive search engine would type "best money maker"
into MediaWiki and get back nothing useful, because no wiki article is titled
"best money maker." So before searching, the query goes through OpenAI with a
few-shot prompt that maps intent to actual article titles:

- *"best money maker"* becomes `Wine`, `Ancient Fruit`, `Starfruit`, `Pig`
- *"how to get married"* becomes `Marriage`, `Bouquet`, `Mermaid's Pendant`
- *"krobus"* stays `Krobus`

Simple lookups stay simple. Vague intent gets expanded into something the wiki
can actually find.

There's also a quick-answer pass: for factual questions like *"when is
Abigail's birthday?"*, a regex heuristic gates a second OpenAI call that pulls
the answer straight out of the infobox. The gate matters, because broad
questions like "how do I farm?" would burn API calls and return nothing
useful, so they skip the pass entirely.

Stack is **Next.js 16 + OpenAI + MediaWiki API**, deployed on Vercel as a PWA
so it installs to a phone home screen.

It's live and I use it almost every play session. More features (saved
searches, mod-pack filters) when the urge strikes.
